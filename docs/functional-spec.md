# Slow LFO — Rev B functional specification

Status: engineering prototype. The 8×8 RP2350 carrier and 22 × 22 mm faceplate opening are separate forks of the earlier RP2040 design; neither is a fabrication release. The 8×8 faceplate still has artwork crossing the opening, and the carrier needs electrical and mechanical resolution. See [RP2350 fork notes](2350-fork-notes.md). The UX contract below is now represented in VCV; the physical firmware still implements the earlier logarithmic interaction. Electrical, physical, and power-cut acceptance on a built unit remain open.

## Product and interface

An 8HP, 3U Eurorack module produces simultaneous, phase-aligned SINE, TRIANGLE, and SQUARE CVs on three 3.5 mm TS jacks. The new controller/display target is the Waveshare **RP2350-Matrix** with its 8×8 RGB matrix. The legacy RP2040/5×5 carrier and schematic remain separate references until the new fork is electrically completed. One five-terminal, detented push encoder is the only control. The display sits above the encoder; the three jacks are below it. No rate CV, sync, output attenuator, or phase-reset control is included.

### UX timing and encoder contract

The revised prototype period range is **1 minute through 64 fixed 30-day months** (165,888,000 seconds, about 5.26 years). The four edit units are literal **minutes, hours, days, and months**: a displayed minute count means that many minutes per cycle, and a displayed month count means that many months per cycle. A fixed month is provisionally 30 days; this keeps periods deterministic without a clock or calendar date. Period is stored as an exact duration in seconds; changing period preserves instantaneous phase and keeps the three outputs phase-aligned.

| Edit unit | Color role | Selectable period | Encoder detent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minutes | Cyan | 1–60 min | 1 min |
| Hours | Green | 1–24 h | 1 h |
| Days | Amber | 1–64 d | 0.5 d; one pixel per day, with the next pixel half-bright at half days |
| Months | Violet | 1–64 fixed 30-day months | 1 month; one pixel per month |

Endpoints intentionally overlap at 60 min = 1 h, 24 h = 1 d, and 30/60 d = 1/2 fixed months. Days and Months both fill the 64 pixels in row-major order from the upper left. In Days, 1 d lights one pixel, 1.5 d lights the next pixel at half brightness, and 64 d fills the grid. In Months, 1 month lights one pixel and 64 months fills the grid. The earlier 25-to-30-day setting gap is gone. The VCV prototype uses an exact-value context-menu label for observation; no text or number is drawn on the hardware display.

At idle, the matrix shows **one static triangle glyph** in the selected unit's color. Its full shape stays visibly colored, while one extra-bright white pixel travels along it to show the shared cycle phase. In UNI (0 to +5 V), the entire glyph and marker occupy only rows 0–3, leaving rows 4–7 dark; in BI (−5 to +5 V), they span the full eight-row height. This is a visual indication of output range; the three jacks continue to output sine, triangle, and square simultaneously. A **short press** (<500 ms) advances the selected edit unit Minutes → Hours → Days → Months → Minutes, displays a four-quadrant color selector with the selected quadrant dramatically brighter, and does not itself change period or phase. Each quadrant fills one 4×4 corner of the matrix, so the selector uses all 64 pixels. Turning the encoder edits the selected unit and shows its full-screen time display. If the existing period is outside that unit's range, the first detent lands on the nearest boundary; later detents move by its listed step. When it is already in range, the first detent moves by one step. No detent wraps at an endpoint. After about two seconds without input, return to the idle glyph. Unit hue remains fixed across selection and time displays.

In Minutes and Hours, the **entire 8×8 matrix** is a circular pie whose illuminated area represents the chosen period as a fraction of that unit's maximum. Boundary-pixel brightness may show fractional fill, but the display is intentionally qualitative. In Days and Months, the matrix is a 64-cell calendar that fills in row-major order from the upper left, one cell per selected day or fixed month. The whole matrix is available in time display because unit identity is carried by color; the four-quadrant selector appears only on short press.

Holding the encoder for **500 ms** replaces the matrix view with the *current* output mode: all upper four rows blue in UNI; upper four rows blue and lower four rows red in BI. Releasing between 500 ms and 2 seconds cancels the preview without changing mode or edit unit. At **2 seconds of continuous hold**, switch **all three outputs together** to the other voltage mode immediately and update the held preview immediately. Continuing to hold causes no further switches; release returns to the usual display. Turning during a press cancels its hold action. Changing voltage mode preserves phase and period, so jack voltage may jump at the switch instant. SINE/TRIANGLE endpoints and SQUARE levels are 0/+5 V in UNI and −5/+5 V in BI. At boot or on return from power loss, a 64-pixel spiral shall show reserve-charge progress from the measured CAP+ voltage to the validated ready threshold; do not use a fixed timer to decide physical readiness. Limit normal matrix brightness and blank it immediately on power failure. During charging, freeze phase advancement and ignore edits as described below.

The present firmware still uses a 60-second to 30-day logarithmic period, MIN/HR/DAY/MO selector, automatically alternating screens, and a moving charging dot rather than measured charge progress. It must be revised after the VCV interaction is accepted. The saved 8×8 faceplate fork retains the MIN/HOUR/DAY/MONTH legends, but its artwork overlaps the enlarged window and needs relocation before fabrication.

## Circuit and pin assignment

J1 is a keyed 2×5 Eurorack IDC connector: pins 1–2 = −12 V, 3–8 = GND, 9–10 = +12 V. Verify physical pin 1, cable stripe, jack pinout, and faceplate fit before applying power. J2/J3/J4 tips are SINE_OUT/TRI_OUT/SQUARE_OUT; sleeves are GND.

The existing review schematic and original carrier name the RP2040 interface pins 1–20 as GPIO0–GPIO15 and GPIO26–GPIO29, pin 21 as 3V3, pin 22 as GND, and pin 23 as VSYS. They allocate GPIO2/3/4 to SINE_PWM/TRI_PWM/SQUARE_PWM, GPIO5/6/7 to encoder A/B/push, GPIO26/ADC0 to pre-isolation buck sense, GPIO27/ADC1 to reserve sense, and GPIO29 to the built-in WS2812 matrix. **This pin map does not describe the RP2350 fork.** The 20-hole RP2350 footprint maps PWM to GP10/11/12, encoder to GP13/14/15, buck/reserve senses to GP26/27, and onboard LED data to GP25; consult [fork notes](2350-fork-notes.md) before updating schematic or firmware. Confirm actual hole geometry, orientation, 5 V power pin, and common ground on a sample.

U3 is a TL074 DIP-14 on ±12 V. C6/C7 are local 100 nF rail decouplers; C8 is +12 V bulk, **not** backup energy. SINE/TRIANGLE retain two cascaded 3.3 kΩ/10 µF passive PWM filter poles. Add one approximately 3.3 kΩ/2.2 µF pole to SQUARE so 50% PWM produces a steady UNI low while output edges remain under roughly 20 ms; measure ripple and edge time before accepting the one-pole tradeoff. Use the fourth TL074 amplifier as a unity buffer for a shared 2.446 V reference made by a 10.5 kΩ/30.1 kΩ divider from U1 3V3. Each waveform amplifier retains nominal 12 kΩ feedback and changes its existing return resistor to 5.76 kΩ from the inverting input to the buffered reference. Nominally `Vout ≈ 3.0833 × Vfilter − 5.096 V` at a 3.3 V rail, giving roughly −5.10/+5.08 V unloaded. Use 330 Ω output series resistors provisionally and verify loaded endpoints and short behavior.

On the pin-header carrier, C9 is specified as KEMET C410C225K3R5TA, a 2.2 µF/25 V axial X7R ceramic mounted vertically with hand-formed leads at 2.50 mm pitch. Check the bent-lead clearance and effective capacitance under voltage bias before accepting the square response.

Use at least 12-bit PWM near 30 kHz. UNI samples map to 50–100% duty and BI to 0–100%; SQUARE uses only 50/100% UNI and 0/100% BI. UNI has about 11 bits of amplitude resolution. Adjust duty endpoints per channel only if measured gain/reference error requires it. At each jack, require nominal endpoints within ±5% into high impedance and 10 kΩ loads; record both load conditions. Measure square edge time and PWM ripple.

## Supply, reserve, and power loss

C11 uses the KYOCERA AVX SCMR14L334SRBB0 footprint (24 × 8 mm nominal board body, 14 mm height, 13.5 mm lead spacing). Its narrow dimension runs across the carrier at the old C11 center, with the existing copper intentionally left untouched for the owner's placement and routing pass. Pad 1 remains `CAP+`, pad 2 remains `GND`; confirm the sample's polarity mark before assembly. The 9 V rating provides headroom; the reserve still charges only from the 5 V buck through D2/R18. The 1-second worst-case hold-up requirement remains subject to measurement.


A through-hole 1 A buck, provisionally RECOM R-78B5.0-1.0, converts +12 V to `+5V_BUCK`. A Schottky diode supplies U1 VSYS normally. A separate Schottky and approximately 10 Ω, at least 3 W pulse-rated THT resistor charge `CAP+` on a KYOCERA AVX SCMR14L334SRBB0 0.33 F, 9 V supercapacitor. A third Schottky feeds VSYS from CAP+. C_HOLD must not discharge into the buck, TL074 rails, or jacks. Check regulator startup/capacitive-load limits and provide accessible +12 V, −12 V, +5V_BUCK, CAP+, VSYS, 3V3, and GND measurement pads.

GPIO26 senses +5V_BUCK **upstream of the isolation diodes** through a divider that keeps its input below 3.3 V. GPIO27 senses CAP+ through a second divider. Sample at least every 2 ms. Set fail threshold and hysteresis after rail measurements. On a falling upstream rail, send an all-off LED frame, capture period/phase/voltage mode, append one pre-erased flash page, and stop nonessential activity. No sector erase is permitted during hold-up. Require VSYS above the validated module operating limit for at least **1 second** after detection, including worst-case measured dark current, capacitance, ESR, aging, and diode drops.

At 10 Ω and nominal 0.33 F, the ideal charge time constant is 3.3 seconds. With an assumed 4.7 V charge ceiling, the ideal time to a provisional 4.55 V reserve-ready threshold is about **11.4 seconds**. The selected capacitor is specified at +30%/−10% capacitance tolerance; diode drop, regulator tolerance, and leakage widen the cold-start estimate to roughly **10–25 seconds**. It may take longer, or never assert, if the charge ceiling is too low. Measure the actual ceiling and set the threshold from worst-case hold-up. At boot restore phase and drive stationary CV values, but freeze phase advancement and encoder edits while the matrix shows a charging animation. The VCV prototype uses a 15-second default and selectable 10/25-second simulations; those are UX durations, not an electrical cap model. Start advancement only after CAP+ is ready. If cut during charging, the previous valid journal record is the fallback.

## Timing, flash, and recovery

Use a 64-bit monotonic microsecond timer and integer/fixed-point phase origin, not a 32-bit millisecond clock or repeated low-precision floating-point additions. Update all PWM duties at least 100 times per second from one shared phase. Period changes preserve instantaneous phase. After storage work, recompute duties from the current timer. Avoid long blocking delays.

Reserve an end-of-flash journal outside the firmware image. Each 256-byte page stores magic, format version, sequence, period, phase, UNI/BI mode, and CRC. Scan for the newest valid record at boot. Checkpoint hourly and once on power fail, never per detent. Erase sectors only under stable power and maintain a pre-erased page. A torn emergency page falls back to the prior valid record and is visibly indicated. If no record exists, use 60 s, phase zero, UNI and write an initial checkpoint once the reserve is ready. Use one core and Pico SDK flash-safe execution so no code or interrupt reads execute-in-place flash during programming.

Phase **freezes while unpowered**. Advancing through an outage requires a backed-up RTC and a different contract. A 64-month setting places much stronger demands on clock stability, flash-journal wear, and long-running power-loss behavior than a 64-day setting; measure and budget those separately before claiming multi-year accuracy. Brownout or corrupt storage must never silently cause an arbitrary phase-zero restart. For the current *unbuffered* two-stage 3.3 kΩ/10 µF sine and triangle filters, an ideal equal-part small-signal model is `H(s) = 1 / (1 + 3sRC + (sRC)^2)`. It predicts sine fundamental gain of about 0.876 at a 1-second period, 0.994 at 5 seconds, and 0.998 at 10 seconds. At 10 seconds the triangle's third and fifth harmonics retain about 0.987 and 0.964 gain. Accordingly, **10 seconds is the provisional minimum hardware target for usable wave shapes**; 20 seconds is a cleaner margin for the triangle. This is calculated, not bench-verified, and does not include capacitor tolerance, output loading, PWM ripple, or actual op-amp behavior. A 1-minute minimum gives substantial margin with the present filters.

### Fabrication and acceptance

Before fabrication: resolve the current carrier's two C11 placement conflicts and eight silkscreen warnings, then repeat DRC after the owner's placement/routing pass; independently review circuit topology and actual part pinouts; compare Matrix, encoder, jacks, supercapacitor, rear-mounted regulator, and keyed cable to the footprints; confirm front/rear component heights and 8HP panel fit. The connected review schematic passes ERC but is board-derived, and the revised faceplate previously passed DRC. Before acceptance: current-limited ±12 V bring-up; regulator heat/noise; both voltage modes on all three jacks into high impedance and 10 kΩ; square edges and PWM ripple; one-day timing comparison; encoder/UI checks; repeated phase-varied power cuts including interrupted flash writes. Scope +5V_BUCK, CAP+, VSYS, and a firmware save marker together to prove at least 1 second of valid hold-up and newest-valid recovery. A dedicated save marker/test pad and accessible rail test pads still need to be finalized for that measurement. Source/build, electrical, runtime, physical fit, and fabrication are separate evidence.
